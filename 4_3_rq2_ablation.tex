\subsection{RQ2: Contribution of Admission and Pacing}
\label{sec:rq2}

\input{tables/tab_rq2_ablation.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:rq2_ablation} separates the contributions of admission and pacing at starting overheat level 4, where the thermal guard begins suppressing optional stages.
\emph{No control} enables neither component, the same configuration as Baseline in Table~\ref{tab:rq1_controller_behavior}.
\emph{Admission only} and \emph{Pacing only} enable one each, and \emph{Full} enables both.
The Captures column reports the percentage of requested captures completed on time.
The \(M\) and \(S\) columns use the same denominator and additionally require the corresponding stage to execute.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Single-component control.}
Only \emph{Full} completed all requested captures on time in all three evaluated Lv4 cells.
\emph{No control} completed \mbox{24.3--42.3\%} of requested captures, while \emph{Pacing only} completed \mbox{28.1--61.4\%}.
\emph{Admission only} completed all requested captures in the S26 Ultra 12MP cell, but 87.3\% and 90.7\% elsewhere.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Limits of admission alone.}
Admission alone achieved full completion in one cell, but skipped most optional-stage opportunities there.
In the S26 Ultra 12MP cell, it completed all requested captures on time, but \(M\) and \(S\) executed on only 29.3\% and 33.7\%, close to the 29.0\% under \emph{No control}.
In the S26 12MP cell, it executed \(M\) and \(S\) more often, on 44.3\% and 51.3\%, but completed only 90.7\% on time.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Coordinated control.}
Compared with \emph{Admission only}, \emph{Full} completed every requested capture on time in all three cells while executing both \(M\) and \(S\) more often in every cell.
Across the three cells, this broader retention came with a responsiveness cost: \emph{Full} activated pacing on 36.7--53.1\% of eligible transitions, with a median positive applied delay of 297--437~ms, equivalent to 37--45\% of the shot-to-shot delay observed when parallel capture was disabled.
Thus, coordinated control combined on-time completion with higher optional-stage execution than admission alone, while retaining most of the responsiveness benefit of parallel capture.
