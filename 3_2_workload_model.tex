\subsection{Workload Model and Online Duration Estimation}
\label{sec:model}

The controller represents Draft processing as an ordered sequence \(\mathcal{W}=(k_1,\ldots,k_n)\) of modeled workload intervals.
Each workload key \(k_r\) identifies a processing stage, an output-size bucket, and the applicable configuration variant.
For capture \(i\), \(\mathcal{W}_{i,j}\) denotes the remaining sequence at admission point \(j\), in execution order and ending with the Draft encoding-and-saving phase.
All duration-valued quantities below are measured in milliseconds.

Let \(\hat p(k)\) be the online point estimate for workload key \(k\).
For any modeled sequence,
\begin{equation}
  \hat P(\mathcal{W})=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\hat p(k_r).
  \label{eq:point-estimate}
\end{equation}
The point estimator learns from completed executions at the workload-key level and shares observed slowdown across keys.

For each executed sequence observed through completion, the controller records a residual: its realized time from the admission point through saved-Draft completion minus the point estimate captured at that decision.
The empirical upper estimate \(U(\mathcal{W})\) adjusts \(\hat P(\mathcal{W})\) with sequence-specific residual evidence and uses a pooled fallback when such evidence is unavailable.
It is a high-side predictor, not a formal timing bound or deadline guarantee.

Each decision snapshots the current model; only completed Draft measurements update learned state, preventing a Draft from revising estimates used by its own earlier decisions.
A workload with neither a learned estimate nor an applicable fallback contributes zero to \(\hat P\).
A zero contribution therefore denotes missing evidence, not an observed zero duration; the controller treats a sequence as unestimated only when its entire point sum is zero.

Equation~\ref{eq:point-estimate} covers only modeled workload intervals.
A \emph{whole Draft} is the profiled interval from the start of Draft profiling through saved-Draft task completion.
The learned estimate \(\hat H\) represents whole-Draft time outside the modeled intervals and is added once per Draft.
During a burst, pacing updates \(C^{\max}_s\) at Draft completion to the largest whole-Draft duration observed for size bucket \(s\).
The learned quantities \(\hat P\), \(U\), and \(\hat H\) persist across bursts, whereas \(C^{\max}_s\) is cleared when the Draft queue drains and is not a bound on future Draft duration.

Remaining-Sequence Admission consumes \(\hat P\) and \(U\); Capture-Availability Pacing consumes \(\hat P\), \(\hat H\), and \(C^{\max}_s\).
