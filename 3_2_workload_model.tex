\subsection{Workload Model and Online Duration Estimation}
\label{sec:model}
Both control modules require online estimates of remaining Draft work. The controller therefore represents each Draft Sequence as an ordered workload sequence and learns workload durations from completed sequences.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Modeled workload sequence.}
The controller models the Draft Sequence for capture \(i\) as an ordered workload sequence \(\mathcal{K}_i=(k_1,\ldots,k_m)\), where each workload key \(k\) identifies a processing stage and its configuration.
From position \(j\), \(\mathcal{K}_{i,j}=(k_j,\ldots,k_m)\) denotes the remaining sequence, ending in a mandatory terminal key measured from encoding start until the Draft image is saved.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Online point estimation.}
For any modeled workload sequence \(\mathcal K\), the controller estimates the duration of a modeled workload sequence \(\mathcal{K}\) by summing its predicted per-key durations:
\begin{equation}
  \hat P(\mathcal{K})
  =\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\hat p(k),
  \qquad
  \hat p(k)=\hat b(k)\,\gamma .
  \label{eq:point-estimate}
\end{equation}
For each workload key \(k\), the controller models its predicted duration as the product of a stable long-term baseline \(\hat b(k)\) and a shared condition factor \(\gamma\).
\(\hat b(k)\) is computed as a cumulative average of condition-normalized durations, reducing the influence of transient workload-level spikes.
The controller estimates \(\gamma\) as the median of measured-to-baseline duration ratios across workloads in the most recently completed Draft Sequence.
Thus, \(\gamma\) captures device-wide execution shifts while limiting the influence of workload-specific fluctuations.
For cold-start cases such as the first capture of an application session, the estimator uses zero until workload durations are observed at Draft Sequence completion.
Histories are cleared only after both the camera application has closed and all queued Draft Sequences have completed.
