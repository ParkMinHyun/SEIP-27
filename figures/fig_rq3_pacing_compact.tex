% Compact coordination-aware RQ3 main-paper figure.  The original RQ3 policy,
% selectivity, and calibration figures remain untouched as historical
% alternatives; docs/rq3-current.md is the authoritative handoff for this pair.
%
% Sources:
%   data/rq3/estimator/draft_pricing_ecdf_*.csv          panel (a)
%   data/rq3/estimator/reserve_error_ecdf_*.csv          panel (a)
%   data/rq3/estimator/scatter_<condition>_<class>.csv   panels (b) and (c)
%
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% What this figure is for, and what it replaces
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Earlier revisions of this figure drew an activation bar chart, a boxes-and-
% arrows "mechanism" diagram, and a stacked bar of the same partition
% Table~\ref{tab:rq3_pacing_compact} now prints as rows.  None of the three
% carried a claim the table could not: two restated table cells as geometry, and
% the middle panel asserted a mechanism in prose set inside a plot frame rather
% than measuring one.
%
% This figure carries the two claims that are genuinely distributional, i.e. the
% two the table can only summarise at P50:
%
%   (a) The controller builds its two estimates by different conventions, and
%       the shapes are what make one of them safe and the other not.  The Draft
%       reserve is the session's observed MAXIMUM for the size bucket, so its
%       error sits almost entirely to the right of zero -- it over-covers by
%       construction, which is the price of the 350 and 374 decisions the table
%       shows pacing acting on although none required a delay.  The backlog
%       clock charges each queued Draft a POINT price, so its per-Draft error
%       straddles zero: right in the middle (P50 +16 and +19 ms), wrong in both
%       tails (P05 -135 and -293 ms).  A central statistic is the correct thing
%       to charge for one Draft and the wrong thing to SUM over a queue, and the
%       separation of the two curves is that argument in one picture.
%
%   (b,c) The sum is taken anyway, and this is what it does.  Each mark is one
%       analyzed pacing decision: the accumulated pricing error of the Drafts
%       occupying the pipeline ahead of it against the error the backlog clock
%       actually carried into the delay formula.  The mass lies on the diagonal
%       -- Pearson r = 0.95 and 0.88 -- so the backlog error IS the queue's
%       accumulated pricing error and not an unexplained model defect.  Reading
%       down the diagonal then separates the outcome classes: the decisions
%       pacing covered sit in the cloud at the origin, and the decisions that
%       left part of the requirement to admission or fell below the mandatory
%       floor are the ones whose queue accumulated 0.6 to 1.3 s of optimism.
%       That is the answer to "why was the backlog under-estimated", and it is
%       the panel the table cannot be.
%
% Panels (b) and (c) are close to definitional and are meant to be.  The backlog
% clock IS the sum of the queued prices, so a relation between its error and the
% sum of the per-Draft pricing errors is expected; what the panel establishes is
% that nothing ELSE contributes materially -- not deadline drift, not a queue
% effect outside the FIFO the clock tracks -- so the whole of the backlog error
% is localised in one place a future revision can change.  The scatter about the
% diagonal is the honest part: the queue is reconstructed offline from the Draft
% timeline rather than read out of the controller's FIFO, so the regression slope
% is 0.84 and 0.79 rather than 1, and the vertical spread is what that
% reconstruction does not capture.  Do not present r as a discovery; present it
% as an accounting result that localises the error, and present the class
% positions along the diagonal as the finding.
%
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Quantities, all signed estimate minus realized so positive means conservative
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
%   Draft pricing error   (beforeWorkloadSequencePredictedDurationMs
%                          + beforeDraftSequenceOverheadDurationMs)
%                         - draftSequenceDurationMs.  What the backlog clock
%                         charged for one queued Draft minus what that Draft
%                         occupied.  Population: every Draft carrying both
%                         terms, 1,984 and 1,904.
%   Draft reserve error   beforeDraftSequenceReservedDurationMs
%                         - draftSequenceDurationMs.  Population: the analyzed
%                         decisions, 1,920 and 1,861.
%   Queued pricing error  the Draft pricing error summed over the Drafts that
%                         had not finished at the decision and started before
%                         the target's own Draft -- the set the backlog clock
%                         was carrying.  Left blank, and the decision omitted,
%                         when any member is missing a term: 1,808 and 1,741 of
%                         the 1,920 and 1,861 analyzed decisions are plotted.
%                         The correlation additionally drops the 1 and 4 whose
%                         queue was empty, where the sum is zero by definition
%                         and carries no information, so it is over 1,807 and
%                         1,737.
%   Backlog error         beforeBacklogMs - realBacklogMs.
%
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Drawing decisions
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Panel (a) is cut at -700 and +1100 ms.  The axis is a reading range; every
% quantile quoted in the table is computed on the whole population.
%
% Panels (b) and (c) share the range [-2.8, 2.2] s on both axes so the diagonal
% is at 45 degrees and vertical distance to it reads as the part of the backlog
% error the queue reconstruction does not account for.  21 of the 3,549 plotted
% decisions (8 and 13) fall outside; they are clipped by the axis, not dropped
% from the data, and the correlations are computed before clipping.
%
% The 12MP panel has no below-the-floor mark because no 12MP decision fell below
% the floor, so its legend lists three classes and the 24MP legend four.  This
% is the same structural zero the table states as a spanning cell, not a missing
% series.
%
% LIMIT.  Every quantity is arithmetic on the realized trace.  The queued
% pricing error is NOT recoverable time and NOT a counterfactual: pacing is
% closed-loop, so a clock that priced the queue differently would have changed
% later arrivals, backlog, admission, thermal state and realized Draft duration.
% The correlation is measured over the analyzed decisions and is not a causal
% estimate.
%
% Regenerate with scripts/rq3_estimator_metrics.py.

  \caption{Where the pacing delay error comes from.  Both quantities are signed
  estimate minus realized, so positive means the controller reserved more than
  the pipeline used.  (a)~The Draft reserve is the session's observed
  \emph{maximum} Draft duration for the capture's size bucket, so its error lies
  almost entirely above zero: it over-covers by construction, which is what pays
  for pacing firing on decisions that required no delay.  The backlog clock
  instead charges each queued Draft a \emph{point} price, whose error straddles
  zero --- accurate in the middle, wrong in both tails.  (b,~c)~One mark per
  analyzed pacing decision: the accumulated pricing error of the Drafts occupying
  the pipeline ahead of the decision against the error the backlog clock carried
  into the delay formula, with the diagonal marking equality.  The clock is that
  sum, so the relation is close to definitional; what it establishes is that
  nothing else contributes materially, and the whole backlog error is localised
  in one place --- a central statistic that is right for one Draft is the wrong
  one to sum over a queue.  Reading down the diagonal separates the
  outcomes of Table~\ref{tab:rq3_pacing_compact}: the decisions pacing covered
  sit in the cloud near the origin, whereas the decisions that left part of the
  requirement to admission, and the 14 that fell below the mandatory floor, are
  those whose queue accumulated $0.6$--$1.3$\,s of optimism.  No 12MP decision
  fell below the floor, so panel~(b) carries no such mark.  The axes are reading
  ranges: 21 of the 3{,}549 plotted decisions fall outside and are clipped by
  the axis rather than dropped, and the correlations are computed before
  clipping.  All of this is arithmetic
  on the realized trace; the accumulated pricing error is not recoverable time,
  because pacing is closed-loop.}
