\subsection{RQ3: Admission Decision Quality}
\label{sec:rq3}

Table~\ref{tab:rq3_admission_audit} evaluates admission decisions rather than deployed stage execution.
We audited 3{,}746 decisions under an always-admit model.
Optional stages always executed with the watchdog suppressed, while admission ran in parallel and recorded its decisions for offline analysis.
Each decision is thus scored against its realized outcome: feasible if its remaining sequence completed within the timeout budget read at that decision, and unsafe otherwise.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Decision accuracy.}
Admission was accurate in both directions: it admitted 97.3\% of feasible decisions and correctly skipped 97.2\% of unsafe ones.
Among feasible decisions, only 2.7\% were conservatively skipped, with a median remaining-sequence margin of only 0.7--2.6\% of the timeout budget.
Among unsafe decisions, only 2.8\% were incorrectly admitted, while the 97.2\% that were skipped exceeded the budget by a median of 3.0--3.5\%.
Admission errors therefore clustered near the decision boundary, while correct skips occurred on decisions already beyond the timeout budget.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Unsafe admits and safeguards.}
Only five unsafe decisions were admitted.
With the watchdog disabled, each overran its deadline in the audit.
Figure~\ref{fig:rq3_unsafe_spike_anatomy} examines these misses against the prior capture in the same run.
For M1 and M2, latency rose to 2.32\(\times\) and 1.95\(\times\), whereas CPU time rose only to 1.41\(\times\) and 1.15\(\times\).
The busy-core proxy, defined as CPU time per unit of wall time, consequently fell to 0.60\(\times\) and 0.59\(\times\).
These misses therefore coincided with post-decision latency increases that could not be reflected in the estimate at decision time.

We then used offline trace replay to evaluate how the deployed safeguards would respond to these five misses.
The replay re-applied the safeguard logic to the recorded stage durations, budgets, upper estimates, and admission decisions.
For M1 and M2, the admitted stage exceeded its watchdog budget, 1{,}460~ms against 1{,}202~ms and 1{,}648~ms against 889~ms, respectively, so fallback would trigger before the stage returned.
For M3, S1, and S2, a later admission decision in the same capture would skip an optional stage, after which the measured remaining sequence stayed within the available budget.
These cases expose the boundary of admission prediction while showing that the layered safeguards would contain all five observed misses under the recorded traces.
