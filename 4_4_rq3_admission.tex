\subsection{RQ3: Admission Decision Quality}
\label{sec:rq3}

Table~\ref{tab:rq3_admission_audit} evaluates admission decisions rather than deployed stage execution.
We audited 3{,}746 decisions under an always-admit model.
Optional stages always executed with the watchdog suppressed, while admission ran in parallel and logged its decisions.
Each decision is thus scored against its realized outcome: feasible if its remaining sequence completed within the timeout budget read at that decision, and unsafe otherwise.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Decision accuracy.}
Admission was accurate in both directions: it admitted 97.3\% of feasible decisions and correctly skipped 97.2\% of unsafe ones.
Among feasible decisions, only 2.7\% were conservatively skipped.
Among unsafe decisions, only 2.8\% were incorrectly admitted.
Together, these rates show that admission reliably distinguished feasible from unsafe decisions without systematically favoring either admission or skipping.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Unsafe admits and safeguards.}
Only five unsafe decisions were admitted; with the watchdog disabled, each overran its deadline in the audit.
Figure~\ref{fig:rq3_unsafe_spike_anatomy} examines these misses against the preceding capture in the same run.
For M1 and M2, latency rose to 2.32\(\times\) and 1.95\(\times\), whereas CPU time rose only to 1.41\(\times\) and 1.15\(\times\).
Busy cores, defined as CPU time per unit of wall time, consequently fell to 0.60\(\times\) and 0.59\(\times\).
These misses therefore coincided with post-decision latency increases that could not be reflected in the estimate at decision time.

All five unsafe admits were covered by the controller's runtime safeguards in replay.
The audit records each stage duration, budget, and upper estimate used by the controller. Replay therefore re-evaluates the safeguard logic from recorded runtime data rather than reconstructing the run from a timing model.
For M1 and M2, the admitted stage exceeded the watchdog budget computed at its own decision, 1,460 ms against 1,202 ms and 1,648 ms against 889 ms, respectively, so the watchdog would trigger fallback before the stage returned.
For M3, S1, and S2, another admission decision in the same capture skipped an optional stage, after which the measured remaining sequence stayed within the budget available at that decision.
These misses therefore expose the limits of admission prediction, while all five remain covered by other runtime safeguards.
