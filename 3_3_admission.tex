\subsection{Remaining-Sequence Admission}
\label{sec:admission}

Admission runs immediately before each optional stage.
It calibrates \(\hat P\) with residual evidence to form \(U\), then compares \(U\) with the capture's live budget to admit or skip the stage.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Residual-calibrated upper estimate.}
The point estimate \(\hat P\) cannot fully capture upper-tail overruns driven by variation in on-device runtime conditions and image content.
The controller therefore learns a high-side correction from observed residuals rather than modeling these effects separately or hand-tuning additive margins for individual devices and stages.
Using stored decision-time predictions, only a fully observed remaining sequence \(\mathcal{K}\) with \(\hat P(\mathcal{K})>0\) contributes a residual factor from realized per-key durations \(p(k)\):
\begin{equation}
  \phi(\mathcal{K})
  =\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\max\{\hat p(k),p(k)\}}
         {\hat P(\mathcal{K})}.
  \label{eq:residual-factor}
\end{equation}
The per-key maximum prevents a faster-than-estimated stage from cancelling another stage's overrun and ensures \(\phi(\mathcal K)\geq1\), while normalization by \(\hat P(\mathcal K)\) makes the factor comparable across duration scales.
Skipped or otherwise incomplete sequences are not imputed.

Factor selection uses the exact-sequence history when available and the cross-sequence pool otherwise.
Each factor enters its exact history and the pool with unit weight and follows the shared decay; let \(w_l\) denote the resulting weight of factor \(l\).
Given at least one factor in the selected history, the controller sets \(\tau\) from the Kish effective sample size \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\)~\cite{kish-survey-sampling}:
\[
  n_{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\frac{(\sum_l w_l)^2}{\sum_l w_l^2},
  \qquad
  \tau=\frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}+1}.
\]
The controller sorts the factors in ascending order and sets \(\phi_{\tau}(\mathcal{K})\) to the first whose cumulative weight meets or exceeds \(\tau\sum_l w_l\); with no observed factor, it uses \(\phi_{\tau}(\mathcal{K})=1\).
Factor observations need not be independent; substituting \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\) for \(n\) is an engineering heuristic motivated by the expected \(n/(n+1)\) quantile position of the maximum of \(n\) independent draws from a common continuous distribution~\cite{david-order-statistics}.
The shared \(0.90\) decay controls both recency and, through \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\), how far the selector reaches into the observed tail; equal weights select the observed maximum.
The resulting evidence-adaptive rule stays within observed factors and requires no separate target percentile.
Applying the selected factor to \(\hat P\) yields \(U\), an empirical high-side estimate of modeled stage time rather than a formal bound on total remaining time:
\begin{equation}
  U(\mathcal{K})
  =\hat P(\mathcal{K})\,\phi_{\tau}(\mathcal{K}).
  \label{eq:upper-estimate}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Live-budget admission.}
At admission point \(j\), \(R_{i,j}\) denotes the time remaining until the Capture Timeout deadline for capture \(i\).
The model admits the optional stage exactly when
\begin{equation}
  U(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})\leq R_{i,j}
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \hat P(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})\leq0.
  \label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
The cold-start case falls on the leading capture, whose Draft Sequence starts against an empty queue with no queue wait charged against its budget.
The fail-open is therefore deliberate: it exposes a cold-start overrun where the budget is most slack, and an optional stage that cannot meet the deadline even there must be fixed or disabled, not absorbed by runtime control.
A completed sequence seeds later decisions.

A model rejection of an \(M\) or \(S\) stage adds its effect group to a queue-local set \(G\).
Until the queue drains and clears \(G\), the set overrides model admissions for that group in the current and subsequent Draft Sequences.
The two groups are tracked independently, preventing effect alternation across consecutive captures while preserving timeout headroom.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Watchdog fallback.}
Admission cannot fully anticipate sudden slowdowns after an optional stage begins; possible causes include CPU scheduling contention, memory reclamation, and image-processing variability.
The watchdog therefore limits waiting for each admitted optional stage to
\begin{equation}
  W_{i,j}=\max\{0,R_{i,j}-U(\mathcal{E}_i)\},
  \label{eq:watchdog}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{E}_i\) is the sequence containing only the mandatory terminal key.
On expiration, the current Draft Sequence falls back to the original input for saving; the stage worker may continue alongside later Draft execution, but its result is no longer used.
After that Draft Sequence finishes, the original-input fallback persists until the queue drains.
