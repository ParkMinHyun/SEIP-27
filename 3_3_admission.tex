\subsection{Remaining-Sequence Admission}
\label{sec:admission}

Admission runs immediately before each optional stage, inline on the Draft critical path.
It calibrates \(\hat P\) with residual evidence to form \(U\), then compares \(U\) with the capture's live budget to admit or skip the stage.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Residual-calibrated upper estimate.}
The point estimate \(\hat P\) does not account for the upper-tail overruns driven by variation in on-device runtime conditions and image content.
The controller therefore learns a high-side correction from observed residuals rather than hand-tuning additive margins for individual devices and stages.
At Draft Sequence completion, each remaining sequence \(\mathcal{K}\) estimated at an admission decision during that sequence contributes a residual factor comparing the stored per-key predictions \(\hat p(k)\) with realized durations \(p(k)\).
Only workloads with \(p(k)>0\) enter the comparison, since a skipped stage produces no duration to compare:
\begin{equation}
  \phi(\mathcal{K})
  =\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}:p(k)>0}\max\{\hat p(k),p(k)\}}
         {\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}:p(k)>0}\hat p(k)}.
  \label{eq:residual-factor}
\end{equation}
The per-key maximum prevents a faster-than-estimated stage from cancelling another stage's overrun and so ensures \(\phi(\mathcal K)\geq1\), while normalizing by the predicted sum over the same stages makes the factor comparable across duration scales.
Each new factor enters two histories, one keyed by the sequence that ran and one cross-sequence pool, and every stored factor weight follows the same shared decay.
The controller selects the weighted \(\tau\)-quantile of the factors in the sequence's own history when that history is nonempty and in the pool otherwise, taking \(\tau\) from the Kish effective sample size \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\) over the selected history's current weights \(w_l\)~\cite{kish-survey-sampling}:
\[
  n_{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\frac{(\sum_l w_l)^2}{\sum_l w_l^2},
  \qquad
  \tau=\frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}+1}.
\]
\(\tau\) reflects the expected quantile position of a sample maximum~\cite{david-order-statistics}, selecting increasingly high weighted quantiles (e.g., \(\tau=0.91\) for \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=10\)); downweighting older residuals lets conservatism adapt without a separately tuned target percentile.
Applying the selected factor \(\phi_\tau(\mathcal K)\) to \(\hat P(\mathcal K)\) yields \(U(\mathcal K)\), an empirical high-side estimate of modeled stage time in \(\mathcal{K}\):
\begin{equation}
  U(\mathcal{K})
  =\hat P(\mathcal{K})\,\phi_{\tau}(\mathcal{K}).
  \label{eq:upper-estimate}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Live-budget admission.}
At admission point \(j\), \(R_{i,j}\) denotes the time remaining until the Capture Timeout deadline for capture \(i\).
The model admits the optional stage exactly when
\begin{equation}
  U(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})\leq R_{i,j}
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \hat P(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})=0.
  \label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
The cold-start case occurs only on each application session's leading capture, whose Draft Sequence starts against an empty queue with no queue wait charged against its budget; admitting there seeds the histories used by later decisions.

Once admission rejects an \(M\) or \(S\) stage, that group's optional stages are removed from subsequent remaining sequences until the queue drains.
The two groups are tracked independently, so a rejection in one does not disable the other; this sticky demotion prevents effect alternation across consecutive captures while preserving timeout headroom.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Watchdog fallback.}
Admission cannot fully anticipate slowdowns after an optional stage begins.
The watchdog therefore waits at most \(W_{i,j}\) for each admitted optional stage:
\begin{equation}
  W_{i,j}=\max\{0,R_{i,j}-U(\mathcal{E}_i)\},
  \label{eq:watchdog}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{E}_i\) contains only the mandatory terminal work from encoding through Draft image saving, reserving that time before the deadline.
On expiration, the current Draft Sequence falls back to encoding and saving from a preserved copy of the original input.
Already queued Draft Sequences also use their original inputs until the queue drains.
