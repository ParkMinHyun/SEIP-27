\subsection{Remaining-Sequence Admission}
\label{sec:admission}

Admission runs immediately before each optional stage, inline on the Draft critical path.
It calibrates \(\hat P\) with residual evidence to form \(U\), then compares \(U\) with the capture's live budget to admit or skip the stage.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Residual-calibrated upper estimate.}
The point estimate \(\hat P\) does not account for the upper-tail overruns driven by variation in on-device runtime conditions and image content.
The controller therefore learns a high-side correction from observed residuals rather than hand-tuning additive margins for individual devices and stages.
At Draft Sequence completion, each remaining sequence \(\mathcal{K}\) priced during it contributes a residual factor comparing the per-key predictions \(\hat p(k)\) stored at the decision with realized durations \(p(k)\).
Only workloads with \(p(k)>0\) enter the comparison, since a skipped stage produces no duration to compare:
\begin{equation}
  \phi(\mathcal{K})
  =\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}:p(k)>0}\max\{\hat p(k),p(k)\}}
         {\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}:p(k)>0}\hat p(k)}.
  \label{eq:residual-factor}
\end{equation}
The per-key maximum prevents a faster-than-estimated stage from cancelling another stage's overrun and ensures \(\phi(\mathcal K)\geq1\).
Normalizing by the predicted sum over the same stages makes the factor comparable across duration scales; if that sum is zero, no factor is recorded.

At each completion the controller decays every stored factor weight by \(0.90\) and inserts each new factor at unit weight into two histories: one keyed by the measured subsequence and one cross-sequence pool.
The controller uses the sequence's own history if nonempty and the cross-sequence pool otherwise.
It sets \(\tau\) from the Kish effective sample size \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\) over the selected history's current decayed weights \(w_l\)~\cite{kish-survey-sampling}:
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\frac{(\sum_l w_l)^2}{\sum_l w_l^2},
  \qquad
  \tau=\frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}+1}.
  \label{eq:ess-quantile}
\end{equation}
If the selected history is empty, the controller uses \(\phi_{\tau}(\mathcal{K})=1\); otherwise, it sorts the factors in ascending order and sets \(\phi_{\tau}(\mathcal{K})\) to the first whose cumulative weight meets or exceeds \(\tau\sum_l w_l\).
With one effective sample the selection starts at the weighted median and moves into the observed tail as \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\) grows, so the shared decay controls both recency and conservatism without a separately tuned target percentile.
Factor observations need not be independent; substituting \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\) for \(n\) is an engineering heuristic motivated by the expected \(n/(n+1)\) quantile position of the maximum of \(n\) independent draws from a common continuous distribution~\cite{david-order-statistics}.
Applying the selected factor \(\phi_\tau(\mathcal K)\) to \(\hat P(\mathcal K)\) yields \(U(\mathcal K)\), an empirical high-side estimate of modeled stage time in \(\mathcal{K}\):
\begin{equation}
  U(\mathcal{K})
  =\hat P(\mathcal{K})\,\phi_{\tau}(\mathcal{K}).
  \label{eq:upper-estimate}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Live-budget admission.}
At admission point \(j\), \(R_{i,j}\) denotes the time remaining until the Capture Timeout deadline for capture \(i\).
The model admits the optional stage exactly when
\begin{equation}
  U(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})\leq R_{i,j}
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \hat P(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})=0.
  \label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
The cold-start case occurs on each application session's leading capture, whose Draft Sequence starts against an empty queue with no queue wait charged against its budget.
This fail-open is deliberate: that Draft Sequence either completes and seeds later decisions, or exposes a cold-start overrun where the budget is most slack; an optional stage that cannot meet the deadline even there must be fixed or disabled, not absorbed at runtime.

A model rejection of an \(M\) or \(S\) stage removes that group's optional stages from subsequent remaining sequences until the queue drains.
The two groups are tracked independently, so a rejection in one does not disable the other; this sticky demotion prevents effect alternation across consecutive captures while preserving timeout headroom.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Watchdog fallback.}
Admission cannot fully anticipate slowdowns after an optional stage begins.
The watchdog therefore waits at most \(W_{i,j}\) for each admitted optional stage:
\begin{equation}
  W_{i,j}=\max\{0,R_{i,j}-U(\mathcal{E}_i)\},
  \label{eq:watchdog}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{E}_i\) contains only the mandatory terminal work from encoding through Draft image saving, reserving that time before the deadline.
On expiration, the current Draft Sequence falls back to encoding and saving from a preserved copy of the original input; because the expired stage may still be running, already queued Draft Sequences also use their original inputs until the queue drains.
