    \scriptsize\emph{Notes:}
    M rows are Multi-frame and S rows Single-frame decisions, ordered by growth
    within each group.  M1--M4 were captured under 12MP normal, and M5 and
    S1--S3 under 24MP memory pressure.
    Every column in the first block is a remaining-sequence cost divided by that
    decision's own admission budget: \emph{Prev.}\ and \emph{Actual} are the
    measured costs of the preceding capture and of the event itself, and
    \emph{Pred.}\ is the \emph{upper bound} the model predicted for the event,
    not its point estimate.  The model admits exactly when \emph{Pred.}\ does
    not exceed \(1.0\), so every row here is an admit whose \emph{Actual} then
    crossed \(1.0\) --- by as little as \(0.004\) for S1 and as much as
    \(0.810\) for M2.
    \emph{Prev.}\ is the capture immediately before the event in the same run
    and group, itself a safe admit in all eight cases; for M2 and M3
    \emph{Pred.}\ sits \(0.010\) and \(0.001\) from it, so the model's inputs
    carried no warning before a jump of \(\times1.95\) and \(\times1.93\).
    The last two blocks give the measured quantities whose quotient is that
    latency: the CPU time the remaining node sequence consumed, and its
    \emph{busy cores}, that CPU time divided by its wall time, which is how many
    cores were working on it at once rather than how many the device has.  Node
    wall time accounts for \(97.5\) to \(99.0\)\% of the latency, so the two
    blocks explain essentially all of the increase.
    The work grew moderately everywhere, whereas the three largest spikes lost
    close to half their parallelism while M5 and S3 gained it and barely moved:
    contention for the cores, not a larger workload, is what the model failed to
    anticipate.
    Overheat level, thermal status, and blocking GC time were identical to the
    preceding capture throughout.
    Costs are forced-execution: the audit build suppresses the per-node watchdog
    so that admitted work can be measured to completion.  Under the production
    watchdog, M1--M3, S1, and S3 would instead have been cut at their node's
    budget, so their \emph{Actual} latency is what the admitted work cost, not
    what a shipped build would have spent on it.
