  \textbf{Delay required} is the share of decisions with a positive retrospective
  matched-policy target $d^{*}$: 79 of 1{,}920 and 140 of 1{,}861. Both $d^{*}$
  and its mandatory-work variant $d^{*}_{\mathrm{man}}$ apply the delay rule to the
  \emph{measured} backlog and \emph{realized} Draft duration, so both are
  sufficient retrospective reservation conditions, not timeout boundaries.
  Over those 79 and 140 decisions, \textbf{Activated} is $d>0$,
  \textbf{Mandatory} is $d\ge d^{*}_{\mathrm{man}}$ and \textbf{Full covered} is
  $d\ge d^{*}$; their gap is optional work, which admission owns at each optional stage --- on
  the 26 and 43 decisions in it, admission skipped optional work on that or the
  next capture in 80.8\% and 72.1\%. All 14 below the mandatory floor ran a
  demoted sequence, their worst realized deadline margin was 4.39\% of the Capture
  Timeout budget, and none timed out. Over the 411 and 471 paced decisions,
  \textbf{Backlog overlap} is the share of applied delay that ran while at least
  that much Draft work was outstanding (0 and 8 waits outlast it), and
  \textbf{$d/B$} the median delay as a share of that backlog.
  \textbf{Selectivity:} pacing engaged on 1.4\% and 1.0\% of decisions carrying
  over 40\% of the budget in surplus, against 77.2\% and 69.3\% where a delay was
  required --- ratios of 55.9$\times$ and 71.9$\times$ --- and 21.4\% and 25.3\%
  overall.
  \textbf{Cost:} over the 70 and 69 runs, the applied delay is 18.1\% and 9.8\%
  of elapsed time at P50 and 24.5\% and 29.7\% at P95; 19 and 13 of those runs
  never paced at all.
