Table~\ref{tab:casestudy_selection} summarizes the setting and compares the selected \emph{Full} run on S26 Ultra at 12MP with its peers.
The run started at overheat level~4 under normal memory conditions and was selected to examine pacing activation, relaxation, and reactivation alongside admission skips.
This run performed worse than the peer medians on all four metrics, making the control interactions more apparent.
Figure~\ref{fig:casestudy_12mp} traces these interactions over 30 captures.
The shaded interval marks Baseline first-timeout indices from separate runs.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Control interaction.}
Admission persistently reduced draft work, while pacing selectively delayed capture opportunities under queue pressure.
During the initial pressure buildup, pacing began at capture 5, before the run reached its smallest slack.
Backlog continued to rise more slowly, but slack still narrowed to 2.1\% of the timeout budget by capture 8.

Admission began skipping \(M\) at capture 9, and backlog declined from capture 10.
Pacing continued through capture 12; after capture 9's draft sequence completed and the projected workload decreased, it was inactive over captures 13--20.
With \(M\) still skipped, slack recovered to 27.3\% by capture 15.
As pressure rose again, pacing resumed at capture 21 and admission began skipping \(S\) at capture 23.
Backlog declined from its 63.8\% peak, and slack reached 45.6\% by capture 30.
