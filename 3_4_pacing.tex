\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog and reserve construction.}
Pacing controls future arrivals using time rather than queue length.  Let
\(E\) be a burst-local clock for the predicted time at which admitted Draft
work will leave the worker, and let \(t_i\) be callback-decision time.  The
online backlog is
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i = [E-t_i]^+.
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}
Here, \([x]^+=\max(0,x)\).  Let \(\hat P_i^{\mathrm{snap}}\) and
\(\hat H_i^{\mathrm{snap}}\) be the point sum and overhead stored in decision
\(i\)'s snapshot.  After choosing \(d_i\), the pacer updates
\(E\leftarrow\max(E,t_i+d_i)+\lceil\hat P_i^{\mathrm{snap}}+
\hat H_i^{\mathrm{snap}}\rceil\).
At every actual Draft start, it removes the oldest pending decision and rebases
\(E\) from the current and still-queued work.  Rebasing keeps prediction error
from accumulating unchecked across a burst.

For a Draft with a modeled workload sequence, the representative whole-Draft
reserve is also refreshed at Draft start.  Let \(\sigma_i^{\mathrm{eff}}\) and
\(s(i)\) denote the effective sequence and size bucket stored in the snapshot
used by decision \(i\).  The pacer projects the configured sequence through the
current sticky demotions and computes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C_i = \max\!\left(
    M_{s(i)}-\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted},i},
    \widehat C_{\mathrm{whole},i}
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here, \(M_{s(i)}\) is the burst-local maximum profiled Draft interval for that
size (with the largest maximum among observed size buckets as a cold fallback),
\(\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted},i}\) is the point cost removed by sticky
demotion, and
\(\widehat C_{\mathrm{whole},i}=\hat P(\sigma_i^{\mathrm{eff}})+\hat H\)
is the effective whole-Draft floor, with both estimates sampled at that
Draft-start refresh.  The coupling is
explicitly asymmetric.  Admission changes later pacing through \(G\) and
\(\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted}}\), but cannot revise an issued delay.  Pacing
passes no numeric state to admission.  Its immediate path to later admission is
through changed arrival time, queue wait, and hence \(R_{i,j}\).  Over subsequent
captures, the altered drain and execution trajectory can also change burst and
learned predictor state.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay computation.}
Let \(T_i\) be the remaining window on the newest capture deadline committed
to the Draft pipeline.  The runtime accessor confines it to the Capture
Timeout window and uses a fresh full window before a deadline is available.
We retain \(\max(0,T_i)\) in the equation to make the expired-window boundary
explicit.  The computed delay is
\begin{equation}
  d_i = \max\!\left(
    0,
    \left\lceil
      \frac{\hat B_i+2\hat C_i-\max(0,T_i)}{2}
    \right\rceil
  \right).
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
The two occurrences of \(2\) implement one deployment constant \(K=2\):
\(K\hat C_i\) sets a two-Draft horizon, while division by \(K\) applies a
\(1/K\) gain to its positive projected deficit.  The horizon and gain are
therefore tied, not tuned independently or by mode.  This heuristic limits
user-visible delay and leaves admission to skip optional work based on its live
budget; it neither passes a numeric share of the deficit to admission nor
constitutes a fixed-point derivation.
Because \(T_i\geq0\), Equation~\ref{eq:pacing} gives the decision-local bound
\(d_i\leq\lceil(\hat B_i+2\hat C_i)/2\rceil\); it is not a global latency cap
because \(\hat B_i\) and \(\hat C_i\) depend on burst state.
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} evaluates the resulting responsiveness cost.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Boundary and coordinated paths.}
Callbacks before the first Draft-start snapshot proceed without a pacing
decision; a nonpositive deficit produces a recorded zero delay.  A Draft with
no modeled workload sequence still consumes the oldest pending decision and
rebases the clock, but retains the prior reserve snapshot.  When the Draft
queue drains, both pacing state and sticky demotions are cleared.  For an
\textsc{Optional} head not already demoted, a suffix whose empirical upper estimate
fits is admitted; otherwise the eligible group joins \(G\).  The next modeled
Draft-start refresh recomputes \(\hat C\) for the reduced workload shape, and
any positive residual
\(\hat B_i+2\hat C_i-\max(0,T_i)\) determines a future callback delay through
Equation~\ref{eq:pacing}.
All hatted terms in Equation~\ref{eq:pacing} are online quantities.  RQ3
separately reconstructs a retrospective matched-policy target from realized
traces; that target is not a runtime input, physical minimum, or causal
counterfactual.  Coefficient sensitivity likewise requires matched runs or a
validated closed-loop replay because changing \(K\) alters subsequent system
state.
