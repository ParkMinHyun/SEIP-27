\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing runs when the framework becomes ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable}, using predicted Draft-worker occupancy to decide whether to expose the next capture opportunity immediately or after a delay.
It delays the callback when the predicted backlog together with additional Draft work would exceed the newest committed capture's remaining Capture Timeout budget.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
While a Draft Sequence is executing, subsequent capture requests can continue to arrive, each triggering a pacing decision before its Draft Sequence is queued.
Because queued Draft Sequences vary in stage composition and processing cost, pacing tracks backlog as predicted processing time rather than queue depth.
It maintains \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), a predicted queue-drain time obtained by accumulating the sequence-level point estimates \(\hat P\) of Draft Sequences represented in the backlog.
At time \(t_i\), pacing estimates the backlog as
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max\{0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i\}.
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay sizing.}
Pacing charges the predicted backlog \(\hat B_i\) and twice the per-Draft-Sequence high-side reserve \(\hat C\) against the newest committed capture's remaining Capture Timeout budget \(T_i\), and computes
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max\{0,\hat B_i+2\hat C-T_i\}}{2}
  \right\rceil.
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
The \(2\hat C\) term reserves processing time for two Draft Sequences beyond the predicted backlog: one queued after decision \(i\) and one for the capture enabled by the callback being paced.
The divisor assigns half of any positive deficit to callback delay, limiting user-perceived delay while admission independently responds to deadline pressure within the active Draft Sequence.

Before the first Draft Sequence starts, \(\hat C\) is unavailable, so pacing delivers the callback immediately.
Thereafter, pacing refreshes \(\hat C\) at each Draft Sequence start as a whole-sequence high-side reserve.
Unlike \(U\), which estimates the remaining time of an in-progress Draft Sequence, \(\hat C\) combines completed whole-sequence durations with the current sequence prediction.
At each start, pacing evaluates the workload sequence \(\mathcal K\) under admission's current sticky demotion and refreshes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}_s-\Delta\hat P,\;
    \hat P(\mathcal{K})
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here \(\Delta\hat P\) is the predicted cost of optional stages removed by the current demotion state, and is zero when none are removed.
\(C^{\max}_s\) is the largest completed Draft Sequence duration observed since the queue last drained for output-size bucket \(s\); an unseen bucket uses the largest observed bucket maximum.
Subtracting \(\Delta\hat P\) adjusts the observed maximum for the current demotion.
The outer maximum floors the corrected observation at the current prediction \(\hat P(\mathcal K)\), preventing \(\hat C\) from under-pricing the sequence.
If no Draft Sequence has completed since the drain, the prediction alone determines \(\hat C\).
Thus, \(\hat C\) is an empirical reserve, not a bound.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Provisional backlog accounting.}
After \(d_i\) is determined, pacing extends the clock with one provisional estimate for the Draft Sequence that will be queued after the decision:
\begin{equation}
  t^{\mathrm{end}}
  \gets
  \max\{t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i\}
  +\lceil\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\rceil.
  \label{eq:clock-update}
\end{equation}
Because its workload sequence is not yet resolved, pacing uses \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\), the point estimate of the most recently started Draft Sequence, as the provisional charge.
The \(\max\) term anchors the accounting horizon at \(t_i+d_i\) when the current clock would drain earlier; otherwise, the new charge extends the existing backlog.
Several pacing decisions can accumulate such provisional charges before the next Draft Sequence starts.

At each Draft Sequence start, pacing resolves its workload sequence and point estimate, then rebases \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) from the actual start time.
The starting Draft Sequence is charged at its resolved estimate, and every remaining provisional charge is repriced using that latest estimate.
This estimate becomes \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) for subsequent pacing decisions.
Rebasing removes accumulated timing error, while repricing prevents stale estimates from propagating through the queued backlog.
