\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing runs when the framework is ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable}, controlling future arrivals using predicted time rather than queue length.
It charges the predicted Draft-worker backlog and an admission-aware reserve against the newest committed capture's remaining deadline window, then delays the callback in proportion to any positive deficit.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
Let \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) be the queue-local predicted completion time maintained by pacing.
At callback decision \(i\), let \(t_i\) be the decision time, \(\hat Q_i\) the current complete-Draft-Sequence estimate and \(d_i\) the delay computed below.
Pacing reads the backlog, records the decision in a FIFO, and advances the clock as
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max(0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i),\quad
  t^{\mathrm{end}}\gets
  \max(t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i)+\lceil\hat Q_i\rceil .
  \label{eq:backlog-clock-update}
\end{equation}
The maxima clamp elapsed backlog to zero and keep the updated clock no earlier than the callback release.
At each modeled Draft start, pacing refreshes \(\hat C\), consumes the oldest FIFO decision if present, and rebases \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) from the starting and queued Draft Sequences, limiting accumulated prediction error.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Admission-aware reserve.}
The refresh applies the queue-local group set \(G\) from Section~\ref{sec:admission} to obtain workload sequence \(\mathcal{K}_i^G\), whose output-size bucket is \(s\), and computes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C_i = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}_s-\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted},i},\;
    \hat Q(\mathcal{K}_i^G)
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here, \(C^{\max}_s\) is the largest completed Draft Sequence duration observed for \(s\) since the queue last drained, and \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted},i}\) estimates the duration of the stages excluded by \(G\).
An unseen \(s\) uses the largest observed bucket maximum, or zero before any completion.
Equation~\ref{eq:reserve} adjusts the observed maximum for excluded stages without falling below the current complete-sequence prediction; \(\hat C_i\) is therefore an empirical reserve rather than a bound.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay computation.}
Let \(T_i\) be the newest committed capture's remaining deadline window, confined to the Capture Timeout window and set to a full window before a deadline is available.
Pacing computes
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max(0,\hat B_i+2\hat C_i-T_i)}{2}
  \right\rceil .
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
A nonpositive projected deficit records a zero-delay decision.
The factor \(2\) covers the Draft Sequence that begins after the decision and the future Draft Sequence enabled by the callback; as the divisor, it applies only half of any positive deficit to limit user-perceived delay.
Together with admission's independent live-budget test, this is a coordination heuristic rather than a fixed-point derivation or an optimality claim.
Because changing the factor also changes later arrivals and controller state, another setting requires matched runs or a validated closed-loop replay, not rescaling recorded delays.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Boundary and coordination.}
Before the first modeled Draft start establishes \(\hat C\), the callback is delivered immediately without a pacing decision.
An unmodeled Draft start still consumes its FIFO entry and rebases \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) without refreshing \(\hat C\); a queue drain clears both pacing state and \(G\).
Admission affects later pacing through \(G\): the next modeled Draft start can lower \(\hat C\) and subsequent delays.
Pacing passes no numerical deficit to admission; it instead shifts the next capture opportunity, changing queue wait and hence \(R_{i,j}\).
