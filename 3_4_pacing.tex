\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing runs when the framework becomes ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable}, using predicted Draft-worker occupancy to decide whether to expose the next capture opportunity immediately or after a delay.
It delays the callback when the predicted backlog together with additional Draft work would exceed the newest committed capture's remaining Capture Timeout budget.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
While a Draft Sequence is executing, subsequent capture requests can continue to arrive, each triggering a pacing decision before its Draft Sequence is queued.
Because queued Draft Sequences vary in stage composition and processing cost, pacing tracks backlog as predicted processing time rather than queue depth.
It maintains \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), a predicted queue-drain time obtained by accumulating the sequence-level point estimates \(\hat P\) of Draft Sequences represented in the backlog.
At time \(t_i\), pacing estimates the backlog as
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max\{0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i\}.
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay sizing.}
Pacing charges the predicted backlog \(\hat B_i\) and twice the per-Draft-Sequence high-side reserve \(\hat C\) against the newest committed capture's remaining Capture Timeout budget \(T_i\), and computes
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max\{0,\hat B_i+2\hat C-T_i\}}{2}
  \right\rceil.
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
The \(2\hat C\) term reserves processing time for two Draft Sequences beyond the predicted backlog: one queued after decision \(i\) and one for the capture enabled by the callback being paced.
The divisor heuristically assigns half of any positive deficit to callback delay, limiting user-perceived delay while admission independently responds to deadline pressure within the active Draft Sequence.

Whereas \(U\) estimates the active Draft Sequence's remaining stage time, \(\hat C\) is a duration reserve for a whole Draft Sequence.
At each Draft Sequence start, pacing evaluates \(\mathcal K\) under admission's current demotion state and computes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}_s-\hat P_{\mathrm{demoted}},\;
    \hat P(\mathcal{K})
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\hat P_{\mathrm{demoted}}\) is the total predicted duration of optional stages excluded by that demotion and is zero when none are excluded.
\(C^{\max}_s\) is the largest completed Draft Sequence duration observed since the queue last drained for output-size bucket \(s\); an unseen bucket uses the largest observed bucket maximum.
The outer maximum floors \(\hat C\) at the current sequence prediction; before the first Draft Sequence completes after a queue drain, \(\hat P(\mathcal K)\) alone determines the reserve.
Thus, \(\hat C\) is an empirical reserve, not a bound.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Provisional backlog accounting.}
Following a queue drain, pacing releases callbacks without delay and makes no provisional backlog charge until a Draft Sequence starts.
Thereafter, each pacing decision \(i\) extends \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) for the Draft Sequence that will be queued after the decision:
\begin{equation}
  t^{\mathrm{end}}
  \gets
  \max\{t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i\}
  +\lceil\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\rceil.
  \label{eq:clock-update}
\end{equation}
The point estimate of the most recently started Draft Sequence, \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\), serves as a proxy for the unresolved workload sequence's duration.
If the predicted queue-drain time precedes \(t_i+d_i\), the \(\max\) anchors the added estimate at the callback release time because the next capture cannot be requested earlier; otherwise, the estimate extends the existing backlog.
Several pacing decisions may extend \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) between Draft Sequence starts.

At each Draft Sequence start, pacing resolves the sequence entering execution and rebases \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) from the actual start time.
It charges that sequence at its resolved point estimate and reprices the remaining sequences using their respective current estimates.
The resolved estimate becomes \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) for subsequent pacing decisions.
Rebasing removes accumulated timing error, while repricing prevents stale estimates from propagating through the queued backlog.
