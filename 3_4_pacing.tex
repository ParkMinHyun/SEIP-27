\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing runs when the framework becomes ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable}, controlling future arrivals using predicted time rather than queue depth.
It delays the callback when predicted Draft-worker pressure threatens the newest committed capture's deadline.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
Under parallel capture a callback can be released before the preceding capture's Draft Sequence starts, so pacing predicts the backlog.
Draft Sequences differ in which stages they run and in what those stages cost, so the backlog accumulates one estimate per pending Draft Sequence.
Pacing therefore maintains \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), the predicted time at which the queue drains; for pacing decision \(i\) taken at \(t_i\), the backlog \(\hat B_i\) is the time remaining until then:
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max(0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i).
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}
Because the Draft Sequence of the capture enabled by the callback is not yet known, pacing then advances \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) using \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) from the most recently started Draft Sequence:
\begin{equation}
  t^{\mathrm{end}}\gets\max(t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i)+\lceil\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\rceil .
  \label{eq:clock-update}
\end{equation}
Here \(t_i+d_i\) is the callback release time, where \(d_i\) is computed by Equation~\ref{eq:pacing} after \(\hat B_i\) is read.
If that release falls after the predicted queue-drain time, the maximum anchors the added estimate there, since the capture cannot be requested before the callback is released.
At each Draft Sequence start whose workload sequence the controller can price, pacing rebases \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) from the actual start using the predicted durations of that sequence and those still queued.
This rebase discards timing error from completed sequences rather than carrying it across captures.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay sizing.}
Pacing charges the predicted backlog \(\hat B_i\) and a per-Draft-Sequence reserve \(\hat C\) against \(T_i\), the newest committed capture's remaining Capture Timeout window, and computes
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max(0,\hat B_i+2\hat C-T_i)}{2}
  \right\rceil .
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
By the time a decision runs, the capture the previous callback enabled is committed; because Draft Sequences execute in arrival order, the newest committed capture is therefore the last one accounted for by \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), which predicts when its Draft Sequence completes.
Thus, \(T_i-\hat B_i\) is the slack predicted to remain on that capture's deadline when the queue drains.

The backlog clock can use point predictions because it is rebased at each such Draft Sequence start, whereas a committed delay is not revised.
Pacing therefore uses \(\hat C\) as a high-side reserve for a Draft Sequence that starts only after the backlog drains, when on-device runtime conditions may have degraded further.
Unlike \(U\), which calibrates the remaining suffix of an in-progress Draft Sequence, \(\hat C\) draws on completed whole-sequence durations.
At each such Draft Sequence start, pacing applies admission's queue-local group set \(G\) to the starting workload sequence \(\mathcal{K}\), yielding \(\mathcal{K}^G\), and refreshes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}_s-\bigl[\hat P(\mathcal{K})-\hat P(\mathcal{K}^G)\bigr],\;
    \hat P(\mathcal{K}^G)
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here \(C^{\max}_s\) is the largest completed Draft Sequence duration observed since the queue last drained for the output-size bucket \(s\) of \(\mathcal{K}^G\); an unseen bucket uses the largest observed bucket maximum.
The subtraction removes the predicted cost of stages excluded by \(G\), while the outer maximum keeps \(\hat C\) no smaller than \(\hat P(\mathcal{K}^G)\).
If no completed duration is yet available, this prediction alone determines \(\hat C\); before the first refresh, pacing delivers the callback without delay.
Thus, \(\hat C\) is an empirical reserve rather than a bound.

Beyond the predicted drain, the factor \(2\) requires \(T_i-\hat B_i\) to cover two future Draft Sequences: one for the capture enabled by the delayed callback and one for the following capture.
The divisor assigns only half of any positive deficit to callback delay, reducing user-perceived delay while admission independently responds to remaining deadline pressure at each optional stage.
Together they form a coordination heuristic rather than a fixed-point derivation or an optimality claim.
