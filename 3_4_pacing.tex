\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing lets the draft worker progress before the application can request another capture.
For capture \(i\), a pacing decision occurs at \(t_i\), when the framework becomes ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable} and before that capture's draft sequence enters the queue.
Capture \(i\) is already accepted, so pacing delays only the next capture opportunity.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
The delay decision needs an estimate of how long the worker will remain occupied by draft sequences already committed to the queue.
Queue depth alone cannot provide this estimate because draft sequences differ in their stages and processing cost.
Pacing therefore accumulates the sequence-level point estimates \(\hat P\) to maintain a predicted queue-drain time, \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\).
At \(t_i\), the backlog estimate is
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max\{0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i\}.
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay sizing.}
The backlog alone does not include the draft sequence for capture \(i\), which enters the queue after the decision, or the capture enabled by the callback.
Pacing reserves a duration \(\hat C\) for each of these two sequences.
At \(t_i\), \(T_i\) is the remaining timeout window of the capture whose draft sequence is predicted to finish at \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\).
This window is a reference for queue pressure; the two incoming captures retain their own timeout clocks.
Comparing that window with the backlog and the two reserves gives the callback delay
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max\{0,\hat B_i+2\hat C-T_i\}}{2}
  \right\rceil.
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
When the projected demand fits within \(T_i\), the callback is released without delay.
Otherwise, pacing applies half of the positive deficit as \(d_i\) to limit user-perceived delay.
Admission independently tests the live budget at each optional stage and can skip that stage if the remaining sequence no longer fits.
This sizing rule is a coordination heuristic, not a fixed-point derivation or an optimality claim; pacing passes no numerical share of the deficit to admission.

The reserve \(\hat C\) must also reflect which stages admission still permits.
Let \(C^{\max}\) be the maximum draft sequence duration observed since the queue last drained.
This observed maximum helps cover slowdowns that the latest point estimate may understate, such as increased throttling before an upcoming sequence starts.
At each draft sequence start, pacing removes the stages excluded by the current demotion state from the configured sequence \(\mathcal K\), obtaining \(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}}\).
It then computes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}-\left[\hat P(\mathcal K)-\hat P(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}})\right],\;
    \hat P(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}})
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
However, the sequence that produced \(C^{\max}\) may have executed optional stages that are now excluded.
The bracketed difference subtracts their predicted duration, so a demoted sequence does not retain a reserve for those stages.
The outer maximum keeps the current point estimate of \(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}}\) as a floor, including when no sequence has completed since the queue drained.
Whereas admission's \(U\) is a high-side estimate of remaining stage time, \(\hat C\) reserves time for a whole draft sequence.
This reserve is empirical rather than an execution-time bound.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Provisional backlog accounting.}
Pacing must update the backlog before it knows the stage composition of the sequence entering the queue.
Following a queue drain, it releases callbacks without delay until a draft sequence starts and provides that context.
Thereafter, each decision provisionally extends \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\):
\begin{equation}
  t^{\mathrm{end}}
  \gets
  \max\{t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i\}
  +\lceil\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\rceil.
  \label{eq:clock-update}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) is the point estimate of the most recently started draft sequence, used provisionally for the sequence that will enter the queue.
The maximum uses the later of the predicted queue drain and callback release as a forecasting anchor; it does not constrain when the already accepted capture can start.
Several decisions may extend this prediction before another draft sequence starts.

When that sequence starts, pacing resolves its stage composition and reconstructs \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) from the current queue state.
It uses the resolved point estimate for the starting sequence and current estimates for the sequences still queued.
The resolved estimate also replaces \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) for subsequent decisions.
Reconstructing the backlog corrects accumulated timing error and refreshes the estimates used by subsequent decisions.
