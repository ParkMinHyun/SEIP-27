\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing runs when the framework becomes ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable}, using predicted Draft-worker occupancy to decide whether to expose the next capture opportunity immediately or delay it.
It delays the callback when the predicted backlog and additional Draft work exceed the live remaining timeout budget.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
While a Draft Sequence is executing, subsequent capture requests can continue to arrive, each triggering a pacing decision before its Draft Sequence is queued.
Because queued Draft Sequences differ in stage composition and processing cost, queue depth alone does not reflect their processing-time backlog.
Pacing therefore maintains \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), the predicted queue-drain time obtained by accumulating their sequence-level point estimates \(\hat P\).
At decision time \(t_i\), pacing estimates the backlog as
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max\{0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i\}.
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay sizing.}
At \(t_i\), let \(T_i\) denote the time remaining until the Capture Timeout deadline for the capture whose Draft Sequence is predicted to complete at \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\).
Pacing charges the predicted backlog \(\hat B_i\) and twice the per-Draft-Sequence reserve \(\hat C\) against \(T_i\), and computes
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max\{0,\hat B_i+2\hat C-T_i\}}{2}
  \right\rceil.
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
The resulting \(d_i\) is applied to the next capture opportunity.
The \(2\hat C\) term reserves processing time for two Draft Sequences beyond the predicted backlog: one entering the queue after \(t_i\) and one for the capture enabled by the paced callback.
The divisor assigns half of any positive deficit to callback delay, limiting user-perceived delay while admission independently responds to deadline pressure within the active Draft Sequence.

Whereas \(U\) estimates the active Draft Sequence's remaining stage time, \(\hat C\) is a duration reserve for a whole Draft Sequence.
At each Draft Sequence start, pacing projects the configured workload sequence \(\mathcal K\) through admission's current demotion state to obtain \(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}}\), and computes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}-\left[\hat P(\mathcal K)-\hat P(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}})\right],\;
    \hat P(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}})
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here, \(C^{\max}\) is the maximum Draft Sequence duration observed since the queue last drained.
A Draft Sequence beyond the predicted backlog may start after thermal throttling has intensified, causing the latest point estimate to understate its duration; pacing therefore uses this observed maximum to hedge against such underestimation.
The bracketed difference subtracts the predicted duration of optional stages excluded by the current demotion state because the completed Draft Sequence that produced \(C^{\max}\) may have executed them.
The outer maximum keeps \(\hat P(\mathcal K^{\mathrm{eff}})\) as a floor and selects it as the reserve when no post-drain observation exists or when model updates have raised it above the adjusted maximum.
Thus, \(\hat C\) is an empirical reserve rather than an execution-time bound.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Provisional backlog accounting.}
Following a queue drain, pacing releases callbacks without delay and does not extend \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) until a Draft Sequence starts.
Thereafter, each pacing decision provisionally extends \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) for the next Draft Sequence:
\begin{equation}
  t^{\mathrm{end}}
  \gets
  \max\{t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i\}
  +\lceil\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\rceil.
  \label{eq:clock-update}
\end{equation}
\(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\), the point estimate of the most recently started Draft Sequence, approximates the duration of the sequence that will subsequently enter the queue. 
The maximum determines when this estimated sequence begins: at the later of the predicted queue-drain time and the callback release time.
Because multiple pacing decisions can occur before the next Draft Sequence starts, \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) may be extended multiple times.
At each Draft Sequence start, pacing resolves the entering sequence and rebases the provisional \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) by reconstructing the backlog from the current queue state.
It charges that sequence at its resolved point estimate and reprices the remaining sequences using their respective current estimates.
The resolved estimate becomes \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) for subsequent pacing decisions.
This rebasing corrects accumulated timing error, while repricing avoids propagating stale estimates through the queued backlog.
