\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing runs when the framework becomes ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable}, controlling future arrivals using predicted time.
It delays the callback when predicted Draft-worker pressure threatens the newest committed capture's deadline.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
Pacing represents the backlog as predicted Draft processing time rather than as Draft Sequence queue depth, since captures differ in which stages their Draft Sequences run and in what those stages cost.
To track that work, pacing maintains \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), the predicted time at which the queue drains; for pacing decision \(i\) taken at \(t_i\), the predicted backlog \(\hat B_i\) is the time remaining until that drain point:
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max(0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i).
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}
After reading \(\hat B_i\) and computing \(d_i\) by Equation~\ref{eq:pacing}, pacing extends the clock with one provisional estimate of Draft processing time for the capture the callback will enable:
\begin{equation}
  t^{\mathrm{end}}\gets\max(t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i)+\lceil\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\rceil .
  \label{eq:clock-update}
\end{equation}
Because the enabled capture's workload sequence is not yet known and the capture cannot be requested before callback release, the update uses the point estimate \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) from the most recently started Draft Sequence and anchors the extension at \(t_i+d_i\) whenever the clock drains earlier.
Several decisions may extend the clock before the next Draft Sequence starts.
At each Draft Sequence start, pacing rebases \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) from the actual start using its resolved point estimate and the remaining stored provisional estimates, discarding completed-sequence timing error without repricing those estimates.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay sizing.}
Pacing charges the predicted backlog \(\hat B_i\) and a per-Draft-Sequence reserve \(\hat C\) against the newest committed capture's remaining Capture Timeout window \(T_i\), and computes
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max(0,\hat B_i+2\hat C-T_i)}{2}
  \right\rceil .
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
By the time a decision runs, the capture the previous callback enabled is committed; because Draft Sequences execute in arrival order, the newest committed capture is therefore the last one accounted for by \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), which predicts when its Draft Sequence completes.
Thus, \(T_i-\hat B_i\) is the slack predicted to remain on that capture's deadline when the queue drains.

Pacing uses \(\hat C\) as a high-side reserve for a Draft Sequence starting after the backlog drains, when on-device runtime conditions may have changed further.
Unlike \(U\), which calibrates the remaining suffix of an in-progress Draft Sequence, \(\hat C\) draws on completed Draft Sequence durations.
Until the first Draft Sequence start establishes \(\hat C\), pacing delivers the callback immediately.
At each start, pacing applies admission's queue-local group set \(G\) to workload sequence \(\mathcal{K}\), yielding \(\mathcal{K}^G\), and refreshes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}_s-\bigl[\hat P(\mathcal{K})-\hat P(\mathcal{K}^G)\bigr],\;
    \hat P(\mathcal{K}^G)
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here \(C^{\max}_s\) is the largest completed Draft Sequence duration observed since the queue last drained for the output-size bucket \(s\) of \(\mathcal{K}^G\); an unseen bucket uses the largest observed bucket maximum.
The subtraction removes the predicted cost of stages excluded by \(G\), making the observation comparable to \(\mathcal{K}^G\).
The outer maximum falls back to the current prediction when this correction would under-price \(\mathcal{K}^G\), such as after a recent slowdown or when \(C^{\max}_s\) already reflects the same demotion.
If no Draft Sequence has completed since the drain, the prediction alone determines \(\hat C\).
Thus, \(\hat C\) is an empirical reserve rather than a bound.

Beyond the predicted drain, the factor \(2\) requires \(T_i-\hat B_i\) to cover two future Draft Sequences: one for the capture enabled by the delayed callback and one for the following capture.
The divisor assigns only half of any positive deficit to callback delay, reducing user-perceived delay while admission independently responds to remaining deadline pressure at each optional stage.
Together they form a coordination heuristic rather than a fixed-point derivation or an optimality claim.
