\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog and reserve construction.}
Pacing controls future arrivals using time rather than queue length.
Let \(E\) be a burst-local clock for the predicted time at which admitted Draft work will leave the worker, and let \(t_i\) be callback-decision time.
The online backlog \(\hat B_i\) is what remains of that clock at the decision.
After choosing \(d_i\), the pacer charges that decision's own Draft to the clock with the point sum and overhead its snapshot stored (Algorithm~\ref{alg:controller}, line~\ref{ln:advance}); the inner maximum there is what keeps the clock from running earlier than the delayed release it just scheduled.
At every actual Draft start, it removes the oldest pending decision and rebases \(E\) from the current and still-queued work (line~\ref{ln:rebase}).
Rebasing keeps prediction error from accumulating unchecked across a burst.

For a Draft with a modeled workload sequence, the representative whole-Draft reserve is also refreshed at Draft start.
Let \(\sigma_i^{\mathrm{eff}}\) be the effective sequence stored in the snapshot decision \(i\) used, and \(s\) its size bucket.
The pacer projects the configured sequence through the current sticky demotions and computes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C_i = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}_s-\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted},i},\;
    \hat P(\sigma_i^{\mathrm{eff}})+\hat H
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here, \(C^{\max}_s\) is the largest whole-Draft duration the burst has observed for that size; a size it has not yet observed falls back to the largest maximum among the buckets it has, which over-reserves rather than under-reserves and never exceeds a duration this pipeline produced.
\(\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted},i}\) is the point cost removed by sticky demotion, and the second argument is the effective whole-Draft floor, with both estimates sampled at that Draft-start refresh.
Equation~\ref{eq:reserve} makes the coupling explicitly asymmetric.
Admission changes later pacing through \(G\) and \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{demoted}}\), but cannot revise an issued delay.
Pacing passes no numeric state to admission.
Its immediate path to later admission is through changed arrival time, queue wait, and hence \(R_{i,j}\).
Over subsequent captures, the altered drain and execution trajectory can also change burst and learned predictor state.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay computation.}
Let \(T_i\) be the remaining window on the newest capture deadline committed to the Draft pipeline.
The runtime accessor confines it to the Capture Timeout window and uses a fresh full window before a deadline is available.
We retain \(\max(0,T_i)\) in the equation to make the expired-window boundary explicit.
The computed delay (line~\ref{ln:delay}) is
\begin{equation}
  d_i = \max\!\left(
    0,
    \left\lceil
      \frac{\hat B_i+2\hat C_i-\max(0,T_i)}{2}
    \right\rceil
  \right).
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
Both occurrences of \(2\) come from a single deployment constant: as a multiplier it sets a two-Draft horizon, and as the divisor it applies a one-half gain to the positive projected deficit.
The horizon and the gain are therefore tied, not tuned independently or by mode.
This heuristic limits user-visible delay and leaves admission to skip optional work based on its live budget; it neither passes a numeric share of the deficit to admission nor constitutes a fixed-point derivation.
Because \(T_i\geq0\), Equation~\ref{eq:pacing} gives the decision-local bound \(d_i\leq\lceil(\hat B_i+2\hat C_i)/2\rceil\); it is not a global latency cap because \(\hat B_i\) and \(\hat C_i\) depend on burst state.
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} evaluates the resulting responsiveness cost.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Boundary and coordinated paths.}
Callbacks before the first Draft-start snapshot proceed without a pacing decision; a nonpositive deficit produces a recorded zero delay.
A Draft with no modeled workload sequence still consumes the oldest pending decision and rebases the clock, but retains the prior reserve snapshot.
When the Draft queue drains, both pacing state and sticky demotions are cleared.
For an \textsc{Optional} head not already demoted, a suffix whose empirical upper estimate fits is admitted; otherwise the eligible group joins \(G\).
The next modeled Draft-start refresh recomputes \(\hat C\) for the reduced workload shape, and any positive residual \(\hat B_i+2\hat C_i-\max(0,T_i)\) determines a future callback delay through Equation~\ref{eq:pacing}.
All hatted terms in Equation~\ref{eq:pacing} are online quantities.
RQ3 separately reconstructs a retrospective matched-policy target from realized traces; that target is not a runtime input, physical minimum, or causal counterfactual.
Coefficient sensitivity likewise requires matched runs or a validated closed-loop replay because changing that constant alters subsequent system state.
