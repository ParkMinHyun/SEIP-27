\subsection{Admission-Aware Capture-Availability Pacing}
\label{sec:pacing}

Pacing runs when the framework becomes ready to deliver \texttt{captureAvailable}, using predicted Draft-worker occupancy to decide whether to expose the next capture opportunity immediately or after a delay.
It delays the callback when a backlog-based forecast indicates insufficient deadline headroom for additional Draft work.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog tracking.}
While a Draft Sequence is executing, subsequent capture requests can continue to arrive, each triggering a pacing decision before its Draft Sequence is queued.
Because their stage composition and processing cost vary, pacing tracks backlog as predicted processing time rather than queue depth.
It maintains \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), a predicted queue-drain time obtained by accumulating the sequence-level point estimates \(\hat P\) over the Draft work currently accounted for.
At time \(t_i\), pacing estimates the backlog as
\begin{equation}
  \hat B_i=\max\{0,t^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i\}.
  \label{eq:backlog}
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Delay sizing.}
Pacing charges the predicted backlog \(\hat B_i\) and two per-Draft-Sequence high-side reserves \(\hat C\) against the newest committed capture's remaining Capture Timeout window \(T_i\), and computes
\begin{equation}
  d_i=
  \left\lceil
    \frac{\max\{0,\hat B_i+2\hat C-T_i\}}{2}
  \right\rceil.
  \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
At decision \(i\), the current capture is already committed, but its Draft Sequence has not yet been added to \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\); thus, \(\hat B_i\) reflects the Draft work accounted for before that decision.
The factor \(2\) extends this backlog by one reserve for the current capture's soon-to-be-queued Draft Sequence and one for the Draft Sequence of the capture enabled by the delayed callback.
The divisor assigns only half of any positive deficit to callback delay, limiting user-perceived delay while admission independently responds to deadline pressure within the active Draft Sequence.
Together, these choices form a coordination heuristic rather than a fixed-point derivation or an optimality claim.

Pacing derives \(\hat C\) from completed Draft Sequence durations as a high-side reserve for work starting after the backlog drains, when on-device runtime conditions may have changed further.
Unlike \(U\), which calibrates the remaining sequence of an in-progress Draft Sequence, \(\hat C\) operates at whole-sequence granularity.
Until the first Draft Sequence start establishes \(\hat C\), pacing delivers the callback immediately.
At each start, pacing applies the queue-local set \(G\) of optional-stage groups retained by admission to workload sequence \(\mathcal{K}\), yielding \(\mathcal{K}^G\), and refreshes
\begin{equation}
  \hat C = \max\!\left(
    C^{\max}_s-\bigl[\hat P(\mathcal{K})-\hat P(\mathcal{K}^G)\bigr],\;
    \hat P(\mathcal{K}^G)
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve}
\end{equation}
Here \(C^{\max}_s\) is the largest completed Draft Sequence duration observed since the queue last drained for the output-size bucket \(s\) of \(\mathcal{K}^G\); an unseen bucket uses the largest observed bucket maximum.
The subtraction removes the predicted cost of stages excluded by \(G\), making the observation comparable to \(\mathcal{K}^G\).
The outer maximum uses the current prediction when this correction would under-price \(\mathcal{K}^G\), such as after a recent slowdown or when \(C^{\max}_s\) already reflects the same demotion.
If no Draft Sequence has completed since the drain, the prediction alone determines \(\hat C\).
Thus, \(\hat C\) is an empirical reserve rather than a bound.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Provisional backlog accounting.}
After computing \(d_i\), pacing provisionally adds the Draft Sequence that will be queued after the decision to the backlog clock:
\begin{equation}
  t^{\mathrm{end}}
  \gets
  \max\{t^{\mathrm{end}},t_i+d_i\}
  +\lceil\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\rceil.
  \label{eq:clock-update}
\end{equation}
Because that Draft Sequence's workload composition is not yet resolved at decision time, pacing uses \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\), the most recently resolved sequence-level point estimate, as its provisional charge.
If the previously accounted work drains before the callback is released, \(t_i+d_i\) anchors the extension; otherwise, the new charge extends the existing backlog.
When Draft processing is slow, several pacing decisions can therefore accumulate provisional charges before the next queued Draft Sequence starts.

At each Draft Sequence start, its workload sequence becomes known and pacing resolves its point estimate.
Pacing then rebases \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\) from the actual start time, charging the starting Draft Sequence at its resolved estimate and repricing every remaining provisional charge using that latest estimate.
The resolved estimate becomes \(\hat P^{\mathrm{last}}\) for subsequent pacing decisions.
Rebasing removes timing error accumulated since the preceding Draft start, while repricing prevents stale provisional estimates from propagating through the queued backlog.
