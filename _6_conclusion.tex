\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The draft sequence provides the initial user-visible image before post-processing completes, and lightweight multi-frame composition narrows its visual gap to the final image especially in Portrait mode.
Under parallel capture, however, the added draft work can consume the fixed Capture Timeout budget as captures overlap on the shared worker.
Production mitigates this risk with a static thermal guard that disables optional stages at a fixed thermal level, even when some captures retain sufficient budget to execute them.
This paper presents the Budget-Aware Draft Controller, which estimates stage durations at run time, admits optional stages against each capture's remaining budget, and paces the release of the next capture when projected backlog threatens that budget.
Across every evaluated device, capture condition, and starting overheat level, the controller completed the 30-capture horizon without an observed Capture Timeout, whereas the unguarded baseline timed out in nearly all of them.
Neither admission nor pacing alone achieved both on-time completion and optional-stage execution.
These results show that coordinating workload and arrival control can retain optional stages while maintaining capture reliability under changing runtime conditions.
