\subsection{Design Objectives and Execution Contract}
\label{sec:overview}

We introduce the \emph{Budget-Aware Draft Controller} to prevent cross-shot
Capture Timeout without reverting to uniform suppression of optional Draft
processing.  The controller coordinates two actuators with different scopes.
\emph{Remaining-sequence admission} reduces the service demand of the capture
that owns the Draft worker, whereas \emph{capture-availability pacing} delays
the earliest application-facing opportunity to start a future capture.  The
former may reduce Draft quality; the latter increases the shot-to-shot
interval.  The objective is to retain optional work when the live budget
supports it while making both costs explicit, preserving the existing
single-worker pipeline and HAL contract.

Figure~\ref{fig:controller-timeline} summarizes the execution contract.  On the
normal parallel-capture path, the pacer compares the time-valued Draft backlog
and reserve with the newest committed deadline, records a callback decision,
and requests release of \texttt{captureAvailable} after \(d_i\).  This changes neither an
active deadline nor a HAL request.  At every Draft start, the controller
consumes the oldest pending decision.  If that Draft has a modeled workload
sequence, the controller also refreshes the reserve.
Admission then rereads the live budget before each optional node and tests its
remaining modeled suffix.  A rejection
demotes the workload group until queue drain; Draft completion updates the
model, and drain clears the burst-local control state.
