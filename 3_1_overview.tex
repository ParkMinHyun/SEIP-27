We introduce the \emph{Budget-Aware Draft Controller} to make the Draft Sequence's lightweight multi-frame composition deployable under parallel capture by adapting stage admission and capture pacing to the live budget.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Controller architecture.}
The controller consists of two framework-local modules.
Figure~\ref{fig:controller-overview} summarizes their coordination.
Under parallel capture, Draft Sequences queue for the single worker, and the two modules act at opposite ends of that queue.
\emph{Remaining-Sequence Admission} controls the active Draft Sequence's service demand: using an estimate of its remaining work and live budget, it admits or skips each optional stage.
\emph{Capture-Availability Pacing} controls when the application can request the next capture: using estimated worker occupancy and the remaining deadline window, it schedules the \texttt{captureAvailable} callback for immediate or delayed delivery.
Both modules share runtime state and online estimates learned incrementally from the observed per-stage and sequence-level durations of completed Draft Sequences.
They operate within the single-worker model and require no HAL or application-interface changes.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Control objective.}
The controller treats avoiding Capture Timeout within the Draft pipeline as a hard product constraint while executing optional stages whenever safely possible.
This constraint spans captures: each capture has its own Capture Timeout window, while Draft Sequences execute sequentially on a single worker.
Let \(\mu_i\) be capture \(i\)'s realized margin at Draft completion, \(\delta_i\) the interval between capture requests \(i-1\) and \(i\), and \(\pi_i\) the interval between their Draft completions.
With a fixed timeout window,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i=\mu_{i-1}+\delta_i-\pi_i .
  \label{eq:margin-recursion}
\end{equation}
The controller's two levers act prospectively on the interval terms of the recursion, which affect the margin in opposite directions: admission can reduce \(\pi_i\) by skipping optional stages but cannot restore consumed budget; pacing can increase \(\delta_{i+1}\) by deferring the next capture opportunity but cannot alter work already in progress.
Equation~\ref{eq:margin-recursion} is an accounting identity, not a control law: neither module tracks \(\mu_i\); each compares its forecast with the live remaining window.
Within this safety constraint, each lever carries a different product cost, and the controller favors user-perceived responsiveness: capture delay irreversibly lengthens the shot-to-shot interval, whereas skipping an optional stage reduces Draft fidelity until the final image replaces the Draft image.
The controller therefore implements a deadline-constrained, responsiveness-dominant trade-off rather than a global cost optimization.
