\subsection{Design Objectives and Execution Contract}
\label{sec:overview}

We introduce the \emph{Budget-Aware Draft Controller} to prevent cross-shot Capture Timeout without reverting to uniform suppression of optional Draft processing.
The controller coordinates two actuators with different scopes.
\emph{Remaining-sequence admission} reduces the service demand of the capture that owns the Draft worker, whereas \emph{capture-availability pacing} delays the earliest application-facing opportunity to start a future capture.
The former may reduce Draft quality; the latter increases the shot-to-shot interval.
The objective is to selectively retain optional work within the live budget while making the trade-off between Draft image quality and shot-to-shot responsiveness explicit.
The design is deliberately framework-local: it preserves the existing single-worker pipeline and changes neither the HAL nor the application interface.

Figure~\ref{fig:controller-overview} shows how the two actuators coordinate across successive Draft Sequences.
On the normal parallel-capture path, the \emph{pacer} uses estimates of queued and upcoming Draft Sequence work, together with the remaining deadline window, to schedule the release of \texttt{captureAvailable}.
At each Draft Sequence start, the controller consumes the oldest pending pacing decision and updates the workload estimate used by later pacing decisions.
Before each optional processing stage within a Draft Sequence, \emph{admission} compares the predicted duration of the sequence's remaining processing with the live budget.
If admission rejects an optional processing group, the demotion remains in force until the Draft-processing queue drains; after each Draft Sequence completes, the controller updates the execution-time estimates used by later decisions.
