Figure~\ref{fig:controller-overview} illustrates how the \emph{Budget-Aware Draft Controller} coordinates optional-stage execution and capture arrivals to enable lightweight multi-frame composition under per-capture deadlines.
The controller consists of \emph{Remaining-Sequence Admission} and \emph{Capture-Availability Pacing}, both initialized with the same shared predictor in Algorithm~\ref{alg:overview}.

Given a shutter request for capture \(i\), \textsc{Capture} records its deadline \(\mathit{timeout}_i\) (line~\ref{alg:ov:timeout}) before requesting capture frames.
When the \texttt{captureAvailable} callback is ready for delivery, the original workflow notifies the application immediately (\(\mathit{delay}_i=0\)).
At this same decision point, pacing computes \(\mathit{delay}_i\) from the predicted draft backlog and remaining timeout budget (line~\ref{alg:ov:pacing}).
The callback is delivered after this delay, exposing the next capture opportunity, \(i+1\), to the application.
    Once draft frames are collected, a draft sequence is created and enqueued for the single draft worker, which processes sequences in FIFO order.

At sequence start, \textsf{ConfigureStages} produces the ordered workload sequence \(\mathcal K_i=(k_1,\ldots,k_m)\) (line~\ref{alg:ov:configure}), where each key identifies a stage and its configuration.
The original workflow excludes optional stages from \(\mathcal K_i\) at overheat level~4 or higher; the controller can replace this fixed cutoff with runtime admission.
Before each optional stage \(k_j\), admission compares the estimated duration of the remaining sequence \(\mathcal K_{i,j}=(k_j,\ldots,k_m)\) with the remaining timeout budget \(T_{i,j}\) (line~\ref{alg:ov:admission}).
After executing admitted stages, the worker performs mandatory encoding and saving, represented by the final key \(k_m\), to publish the draft image (line~\ref{alg:ov:publish}).

At completion, \textsc{OnDraftSequenceCompleted} receives \(\mathcal K_i\) with each stage's execution outcome (executed or skipped).
It also receives the measured draft sequence duration \(C_i\) and the measured durations \(p_i(k)\) of executed stages.
The measured durations update the shared predictor (line~\ref{alg:ov:update}), after which admission and pacing update their respective states (lines~\ref{alg:ov:ad-residual}--\ref{alg:ov:pa-rebase}).
Pacing accounts for optional stages disabled by admission (Section~\ref{sec:admission}) in the workload estimates used to determine callback delays.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Control objective.}
The control objective is to complete each capture's draft sequence before its Capture Timeout deadline and, within that constraint, to favor capture responsiveness over optional-stage retention.
Deadline safety comes first because a single Capture Timeout violation blocks the release.
Responsiveness comes next because added shot-to-shot delay is irreversible, whereas skipping stages lowers draft quality but leaves the final image unaffected.
Let \(\mu_i\) be the time left before capture \(i\)'s deadline at draft completion, \(\delta_i\) the interval between requests \(i-1\) and \(i\), and \(\pi_i\) the interval between their draft completions.
With a fixed timeout duration,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i=\mu_{i-1}+\delta_i-\pi_i .
  \label{eq:margin-recursion}
\end{equation}
When \(\pi_i>\delta_i\), deadline margin shrinks.
Admission can shorten \(\pi_i\) by skipping optional stages; pacing can lengthen \(\delta_{i+1}\) by delaying the next capture opportunity.
Admission cannot recover time already spent waiting, while pacing cannot change work in progress.
