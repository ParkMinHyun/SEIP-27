\subsection{Overview and Control Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[trim=12 10 0 11,clip,width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_controller_interaction.pdf}
  \caption{Coordinated admission and pacing in the Budget-Aware Draft Controller.}
  \label{fig:controller-overview}
\end{figure*}
We introduce the \emph{Budget-Aware Draft Controller} to enable the draft sequence's lightweight multi-frame composition under parallel capture while meeting each capture's deadline.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Controller architecture.}
The controller coordinates optional-stage execution on the single draft worker with capture arrivals, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:controller-overview}.
\emph{Remaining-Sequence Admission} runs immediately before an optional stage in the active draft sequence.
It compares the estimated duration of the remaining sequence with the capture's remaining timeout budget to decide whether to execute the stage.
\emph{Capture-Availability Pacing} controls delivery of \texttt{captureAvailable}, which exposes the next capture opportunity to the application.
It uses the estimated draft backlog and the remaining timeout budget to decide whether to deliver the callback immediately or delay it.
Both modules use duration estimates based on observations from completed draft sequences.
Pacing accounts for admission decisions when estimating how long upcoming draft sequences will take.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Control objective.}
Capture Timeout is a hard product constraint on enabling optional stages.
Each accepted capture starts its own timeout clock, but its draft sequence may wait behind earlier captures on the shared worker.
Let the deadline margin \(\mu_i\) denote the time left before capture \(i\)'s deadline when its draft sequence completes.
For successive captures, let \(\delta_i\) be the interval between requests \(i-1\) and \(i\), and \(\pi_i\) the interval between their draft sequence completions.
With a fixed timeout duration,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i=\mu_{i-1}+\delta_i-\pi_i .
  \label{eq:margin-recursion}
\end{equation}
When completions fall farther apart than requests, \(\pi_i>\delta_i\), and successive captures lose deadline margin.
Admission can shorten \(\pi_i\) by skipping optional stages, but it cannot recover time already spent waiting.
Pacing can lengthen \(\delta_{i+1}\) by delaying the next capture opportunity, but it cannot change work already in progress.
Equation~\eqref{eq:margin-recursion} is an accounting identity, not a control law.
The controller does not maintain \(\mu_i\) as state; at each decision, it compares a duration estimate with the remaining timeout budget.

Within this deadline constraint, the controller prioritizes user-perceived responsiveness.
Capture delay lengthens the shot-to-shot interval and cannot be recovered, whereas the fidelity cost of a skipped stage ends when the final image replaces the draft image.
The controller therefore implements a deadline-constrained, responsiveness-dominant trade-off between optional-stage execution and shot-to-shot delay rather than a global cost optimization.
