\subsection{Control Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

The controller's goal is to keep lightweight multi-frame Draft composition available whenever it is safe, and cross-shot Capture Timeout is the safety constraint that defines when it is not.
Under deadline pressure, the controller can pay two costs to hold that constraint: a delayed next capture and a skipped optional Draft stage.
The delay is the more expensive by product policy, because it is directly visible to the user, whereas a skipped stage affects only the early Draft image and is normally masked by final processing.

Each capture starts a fresh Capture Timeout clock, but the single Draft worker serializes Draft Sequences, so a predecessor can occupy the worker past the next capture request and consume budget that would otherwise be available to that successor.
Capture \(i\) must complete its Draft path within a budget measured from its request; let \(\mu_i\) be its realized margin at completion, \(\delta_i\) the interval between capture requests \(i-1\) and \(i\), and \(\pi_i\) the interval between their Draft-path completions.
For successive captures,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i=\mu_{i-1}+\delta_i-\pi_i .
  \label{eq:margin-recursion}
\end{equation}
Only two quantities move the margin, and each module acts on one: admission reduces \(\pi_i\) by suppressing optional work, pacing increases \(\delta_{i+1}\) by deferring the next capture opportunity.

Neither module passes a residual budget to the other: admission independently tests the live budget at each optional node, and pacing converts only a fixed fraction of its projected deficit into delay.
The result is a safety-constrained, responsiveness-dominant trade-off, not the optimum of a cost model: the controller computes no quality utility and solves no global optimization.

Equation~\ref{eq:margin-recursion} is an accounting identity, not tracked control state; each module instead compares a decision-time forecast against the deadline window still observable to it.
Because neither \(\delta\) nor \(\pi\) is known when the shaping decision is made, and each decision changes later queueing and thermal conditions, keeping \(\mu_i>0\) is a design goal rather than a guaranteed invariant.
