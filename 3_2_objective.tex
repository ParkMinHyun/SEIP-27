\subsection{Control Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

Avoiding Capture Timeout within the Draft pipeline is a hard constraint while preserving lightweight multi-frame composition.
Subject to this constraint, the controller trades off two product costs: delaying the next capture opportunity and skipping optional Draft work.
It favors responsiveness because capture delay irreversibly increases shot-to-shot latency, whereas skipping optional work temporarily reduces Draft fidelity until post-processing replaces the Draft image.

To expose how these costs shape deadline pressure across captures, we use realized deadline margin as an accounting view.
For capture \(i\), let \(\mu_i\) be its realized margin when the Draft pipeline completes, \(\delta_i\) the interval between capture requests \(i-1\) and \(i\), and \(\pi_i\) the interval between their Draft-pipeline completions.
With a fixed Capture Timeout window,
\begin{equation}
\mu_i=\mu_{i-1}+\delta_i-\pi_i .
\label{eq:margin-recursion}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:margin-recursion} exposes the two control levers: admission can reduce \(\pi_i\) by removing optional work, while pacing can increase \(\delta_{i+1}\) by deferring the next capture opportunity.
The equation is an accounting identity, not tracked control state: \(\mu_i\) is realized only at completion, so \(\mu_i>0\) is the safety condition the controller seeks to preserve rather than a runtime invariant directly maintained from realized margin.
The runtime modules instead compare decision-time forecasts with the deadline windows they can still observe.
