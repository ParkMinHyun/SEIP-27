## Paper Goal

- Current manuscript topic: enabling multi-frame composition in a camera draft pipeline under capture deadlines through coordinated admission and pacing.
- **Title approved by the author on 2026-09-22:** `Enabling Multi-Frame Composition in a Camera Draft Pipeline: Coordinated Admission and Pacing under Capture Deadlines`. The title omits `lightweight` to foreground feature enablement and the coordinated control mechanism. Keep `lightweight multi-frame composition` in the body where it describes the actual stage; this is not a system rename or a claim about a new composition algorithm. The system remains the `Budget-Aware Draft Controller`.
- The Draft Sequence was introduced together with the post-processing pipeline; it publishes an early image and provides recovery while post-processing continues.
- The work targets enabling lightweight multi-frame composition in the Draft Sequence for Portrait mode, whose heavy post-processing can then be deferred until the camera app enters the background.
- The proposed system is the Budget-Aware Draft Controller, comprising remaining-sequence admission and capture-availability pacing. Use the descriptive name without a forced acronym and refer to it as ``the controller'' after first use.
- The paper studies coordinated workload and arrival control for tail-latency-based Capture Timeout, not average-latency optimization for a single image-processing stage.
- Frame the work as feature enablement under a safety constraint, not as timeout mitigation. The hierarchy is: enable the draft sequence's lightweight multi-frame composition under parallel capture whenever safely possible → Capture Timeout is the deployment blocker → static thermal gating cannot tell safe captures from unsafe ones → coordinated runtime control of workload and capture arrival. Sections 2.4 and 3.1 already open on this hierarchy; align the abstract, introduction, and conclusion with it when they are drafted.
- Preserve the core research framing unless the user explicitly asks to change the problem statement, contribution, or terminology.

Where a sentence means the event rather than the symbol the prose already switches wording — `the callback` in `3_4_pacing.tex`, `capture-availability transitions` in `4_1_setup.tex` — and that split must survive, so the same referent is never typeset two ways.
- **When drafting `_0_abstract.tex` and `_1_introduction.tex`, prefer `capture-availability callback` to the bare identifier.** The name is Samsung-internal and AOSP Camera2 has no such method (`onCaptureStarted`, `onImageAvailable`), so a monospace token before `2_2_parallel_capture.tex` establishes the HAL context reads as a public API call. Use `\texttt{captureAvailable}` in those two files only where the sentence is genuinely about the framework signal, and keep the typeface rule above if so.
- **Use \(T\) for the live remaining Capture Timeout window in both control modules.** In admission, \(T_{i,j}\) is capture \(i\)'s remaining window at admission point \(j\). In pacing, \(i\) stays the capture index rather than becoming a decision index: capture \(i\)'s request triggers the decision at \(t_i\), \(T_i\) is the window that decision reads for the capture whose Draft Sequence is predicted to complete at \(t^{\mathrm{end}}\), and \(d_i\) applies to the next capture opportunity, which is what connects it to \(\delta_{i+1}\) in Section 3.1. Do not reintroduce \(R_{i,j}\) for the admission-side reading.
- Call the controller's policy a **deadline-constrained, responsiveness-dominant trade-off**. Explicitly name its two competing objectives as **optional-stage execution and shot-to-shot delay** in the introduction, Section 3's control objective, and Section 4's evaluation opening (author clarification, 2026-09-22). Capture deadlines are the constraint, and responsiveness is the priority within that constraint. `timeout > delay > admit` is acceptable when describing the product priority informally, but never translate it into a strict lexicographic optimum: that optimum is the corner solution "delay only as a last resort", which the deployed policy does not implement. Section~4 states the trade-off by name so the four RQs read as its verification.
- Section 3.1 carries the margin recurrence \(\mu_i=\mu_{i-1}+\delta_i-\pi_i\) (`eq:margin-recursion`) and its three symbols in `Control objective.`. **It was removed on the morning of 2026-09-15 and restored by the author the same day in `1fcc4f2`; do not remove it again unless the author asks.** Keep both caveats beside it: the equation is an accounting identity, not a control law, and the controller does not maintain \(\mu_i\) as state but compares a processing-time forecast with the live remaining timeout budget at each decision. Section 3.1 carries no scalar cost objective. A `min Σκ(d_i) + Σ(1-a_{i,j})v_{i,j}` formulation was drafted and rejected for three reasons, all of which still apply: convexity of the delay cost does not imply the split the deployed policy performs, because admission is discrete and its quality cost is a step function; `κ` and `v` appear in neither the implementation nor the evaluation, so the `argmin` reads as ornamental; and `π_i` is a completion-to-completion interval that equals a capture's service demand only while the worker stays busy, which is exactly the condition pacing exists to break. Do not reintroduce anything of that shape.
- Three further claims Section 3.1 must not make, each of which contradicts the implementation or a later subsection: that margin "does not reset" (every capture starts a fresh clock, and the coupling comes from serialized Draft execution); that pacing leaves a numeric remainder to admission (no numeric state passes between the two modules; the role division Section 3.4 gives for halving the deficit is design intent, not a handoff); and that either runtime test is an online form of `μ_i > 0` (`μ_i` is realized only at completion, so the tests are decision-time forecasts against the deadline window each module can still read).
- Sections 3.1 and 3.2 were merged into `3_1_overview.tex` on 2026-08-15. `3_2_objective.tex` is deleted, and the four files after it were renumbered down by one to keep file names aligned with subsection numbers: workload model `3_2_workload_model.tex`, admission `3_3_admission.tex`, pacing `3_4_pacing.tex`, implementation `3_5_implementation.tex`. Labels were not renamed, so `sec:model`, `sec:admission`, `sec:pacing`, and `sec:implementation` still resolve; cite subsections by label, never by hard-coded number. **The 2026-09-15 style review removed repeated explanations between the two run-in blocks; the author then restored the recurrence and the module limits in `Control objective.` (`1fcc4f2`).** `Controller architecture.` explains each module's decision once, followed by the shared duration estimates and how admission decisions inform pacing; it carries no module limits. `Control objective.` keeps this order: the hard product constraint, the per-capture deadline clock and waiting on the shared worker, the recurrence, the two levers each with its limit (admission shortens \(\pi_i\) but cannot recover time already spent waiting; pacing lengthens \(\delta_{i+1}\) but cannot change work already in progress), the identity-not-a-control-law caveat, then the responsiveness priority and the difference in recoverability between delay and skipped-stage fidelity costs. The caveat sits between the equation and the ranking so the responsiveness-dominant preference reads as a product priority rather than a consequence of the recurrence. State each limit only beside its lever; the admission limit also motivates joint control in `2_4_static_safeguards.tex`, and 3.1 restates it as the limit of the lever the recurrence exposes. End the block on the named deadline-constrained, responsiveness-dominant trade-off. Keep `\label{sec:objective}` immediately after the `\subsection` command, before any float or run-in heading, so references anchor to the subsection.
