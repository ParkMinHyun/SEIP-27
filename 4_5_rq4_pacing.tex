\subsection{RQ4: Pacing under Queue Pressure}
\label{sec:rq4}

\input{tables/tab_rq4_pacing_sizing.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:rq4_pacing_sizing} evaluates 3{,}050 pacing decisions from 116 \emph{Full} runs in the 12MP normal condition.
This is the production-default setting without external memory pressure.
Panel~(a) groups decisions by the measured backlog \(B_i\) as a fraction of the Capture Timeout budget.
Panel~(b) reports pacing coverage for captures that finished with less than 20\%, 10\%, and 5\% of the timeout budget remaining.
For paced decisions, \(d_i/B_i\) expresses the pacing delay relative to the backlog.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Queue response.}
Pacing activation increased sharply with backlog, from 0\% below 10\% of the budget to 8.2\%, 45.0\%, and 83.4\% in successively higher bands.
Among paced decisions, median \(d_i/B_i\) decreased from 29.1\% in the lightest active band to 13.0\% and 8.3\% as backlog increased.
Pacing therefore scaled with queue pressure mainly through more frequent activation rather than larger relative delays.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Selective pacing under pressure.}
High backlog alone did not trigger pacing.
In the \(\geq 50\%\) backlog group, 574 of 688 decisions were paced, leaving 114 unpaced.
Of these 114, 58 were observed predominantly at lower overheat levels and were still projected to fit within the deadline without pacing.
For the other 56, an admission skip had already reduced the projected remaining draft work enough to fit within the deadline.

Pacing concentrated on captures closest to the deadline.
Among captures finishing within 20\%, 10\%, and 5\% of the deadline, pacing engaged on 85.4\%, 92.3\%, and 95.7\%, respectively.
In the tightest 5\% group, 133 of 139 were paced.
The remaining six were backlog-underestimation misses at the pacing decision, but later admission skips kept each capture within budget.
Despite this high near-deadline coverage, median pacing delay was only about 10\% of the queued backlog.
