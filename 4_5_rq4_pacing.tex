\subsection{RQ4: Pacing under Queue Pressure}
\label{sec:rq4}

\input{tables/tab_rq4_pacing_sizing.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:rq4_pacing_sizing} evaluates 3{,}050 pacing decisions from 116 \emph{Full} runs in the 12MP normal condition.
Panel~(a) groups decisions by the measured backlog \(B_i\) as a fraction of the Capture Timeout budget.
Panel~(b) reports pacing coverage for captures finishing within 20\%, 10\%, and 5\% of the deadline.
For paced decisions, \(d_i/B_i\) reports the delay relative to the backlog.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Queue response.}
Pacing activation increased sharply with backlog, from 0\% below 10\% of the budget to 8.2\%, 45.0\%, and 83.4\% in successively higher bands.
Among paced decisions, median \(d_i/B_i\) decreased from 29.1\% in the lightest active band to 13.0\% and 8.3\% as backlog increased.
Pacing therefore responded to heavier queue pressure mainly by engaging more often, rather than by increasing delay in proportion to backlog.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Selective pacing under pressure.}
High backlog alone did not trigger pacing.
Of the 114 unpaced decisions with at least half of the budget already queued, 58 were already projected to fit within the remaining budget without delay.
For the other 56, an admission skip had reduced the projected remaining workload enough to fit.
Conversely, among captures that finished within 20\%, 10\%, and 5\% of the deadline, pacing engaged on 85.4\%, 92.3\%, and 95.7\%, respectively.
Their median \(d_i/B_i\) remained within 10.1--11.2\%, indicating that coverage increased through more frequent activation rather than larger relative delays.
All six captures within 5\% that received no delay had an optional stage skipped by admission.
Thus, pacing remained selective under high backlog, while no capture finishing within 5\% of the deadline passed through both controls untouched.
