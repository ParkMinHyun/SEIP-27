\subsection{RQ4: Pacing under Queue Pressure}
\label{sec:rq4}

Table~\ref{tab:rq4_pacing_sizing} evaluates pacing decisions from \emph{Full} runs in the 12MP normal condition, pooled over both devices.
Panel~(a) groups decisions by the backlog \(B_i\) measured from the completed trace, as a fraction of the Capture Timeout budget.
Panel~(b) reports pacing coverage for nested groups with limited slack, where slack is the deadline margin at draft sequence completion.
Pacing activation is the fraction of all decisions in each group with a positive delay; the median delay-to-backlog ratio \(\mathit{delay}_i/B_i\) includes only these paced decisions.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Backlog response.}
Pacing activated more often as measured backlog increased, while its median delay relative to backlog decreased.
Activation was 0\% in the lowest backlog band and rose to 8.2\%, 45.0\%, and 83.4\% across the three higher bands.
Among paced decisions, median \(\mathit{delay}_i/B_i\) decreased from 29.1\% in the lightest active band to 13.0\% and 8.3\% as backlog increased.
The falling ratio describes relative delay and does not imply a decrease in absolute delay.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Selective pacing.}
High measured backlog alone was not sufficient to trigger pacing.
In the \(\geq 50\%\) backlog group, 574 of 688 decisions were paced, leaving 114 unpaced.
Of these 114, 58 had no projected excess over the queue-level budget used by pacing and occurred predominantly at lower overheat levels.
For the other 56, an earlier admission skip had reduced the projected draft work to within this budget.

Pacing was more frequent for captures finishing closer to the deadline.
Among captures with slack below 20\%, 10\%, and 5\% of the budget, pacing engaged on 85.4\%, 92.3\%, and 95.7\%, respectively.
For the six unpaced captures in the tightest group, predicted backlog underestimated measured backlog at the pacing decision.
Later admission skips reduced draft work, and all six captures completed within budget.
Across these groups, median \(\mathit{delay}_i/B_i\) among paced decisions was 10.1--11.2\%.
